Covered today: Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Uttarakhand, Goa and Manipur, the five states going to the polls again in 2027.

Why I built it

Election sites tend to do one of two things: archive old results, or chase the latest news. I wanted a clean reference layer first, one a reader can trust because it stays within what actually happened.

  • Facts only. Maps are coloured by the last election's result. No predictions, forecasts or "what if" widgets, by design.
  • Seat-level detail. Every constituency shows its winner, runner-up, vote shares and margin, plus party-wise tallies per state.
  • Sources you can check. A references page lists official bodies and independent sites that publish Indian election data.

How it was built

  • Constituency boundaries were processed from public map data into lightweight vector maps that load fast, with names cross-checked between the map and results data to catch mismatches.
  • 2022 results were compiled from public datasets and tables, with seat totals checked against the official counts.
  • A static front end on its own domain, with clean path-based URLs so each state can be linked directly.
  • Method notes live on a separate About page rather than cluttering the main view.

What's next

  • Live seat-by-seat results and counting as the 2027 cycle moves on.
  • Election news and stories alongside the data, drawn only from credible sources.
Ginti is an independent project and is not affiliated with any election body or political party. Always check results against the official source.
